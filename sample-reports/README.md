# Two directions, and the folder names are the whole point

```
sample-reports/
  from-the-dealer/    files that come IN  — a dealership's own exports, which DDMS reads
  from-ddms/          files that go OUT   — what a chartered accountant opens
```

These used to sit in one folder. They are opposite directions and mixing them is
how, in six months, nobody can say which file proves what — whether a figure is
something the dealership told us or something we told them.

> **Nothing in either is invented, and nothing is hand-authored.** Every value is
> read out of the database. The order is always **seed first, then export**, and
> both sides are regenerated by a command:
>
> ```
> pnpm run db:export-reports   # from-the-dealer
> pnpm run db:export-books     # from-ddms
> ```
>
> A CSV written by hand for a branch with no data behind it would give the
> product two versions of one dealership, which is the failure it exists to fix.

---

## `from-the-dealer/` — what DDMS reads

Eight reports per **dealer code**, numbered in the order a dealership is
onboarded rather than alphabetically: you cannot make sense of an enquiry
register until you know who the sales executives are.

```
from-the-dealer/
  HMC-DL-0417/    Saraswati Automobiles, New Delhi — five branches, one dealer code
  HMC-MH-1182/    Deccan Two Wheelers — parked (R-43), a handful of rows
```

**Keyed by dealer code and not by branch, because that is how the export
actually arrives.** Hero issues a code to a *dealership*, not to every
shopfront. Saraswati's five branches share `HMC-DL-0417`, so they share one
stock register — a satellite has no code of its own and a service outlet never
will. A per-branch view of the same data is a DDMS report, and it is on the
other side.

| | | Saraswati |
|---|---|---|
| `01-vehicle-stock.csv` | what is standing on the floor, with what it cost and what it is costing | 141 |
| `02-staff-master.csv` | everybody, **including the ones who left** | 29 |
| `03-enquiry-register.csv` | customers, as leads, against the manufacturer's clock | 141 |
| `04-deal-register.csv` | sales — booking to invoice to delivery | 78 |
| `05-registration-register.csv` | RTO files, lodged and pending | 88 |
| `06-job-card-register.csv` | the workshop, against the promise made | 104 |
| `07-part-stock.csv` | spares on the shelf | 67 |
| `08-receivables.csv` | what the outlet is owed, party by party | 50 |

### Three rules that decide what may appear

**Mirror fields only, never decision fields.** A dealer's system knows the
chassis number, the promised date and the invoice number. It has never heard of
`reassignedToEmpCode`, `customerInformedAt` or `rtoChasedAt` — those are what
*DDMS* concluded, and a file claiming to come from the dealer while carrying them
would be the product reading its own output back as evidence.

**Their vocabulary.** `Deal No`, not `dealId`. The whole point of the mapping is
that a dealership's headings are theirs and a model is asked once what they mean;
headings that happened to match our column names would prove nothing. On this
shape all of them are placed by name and **no model is called at all**.

**Their formats.** `dd/mm/yyyy`, `₹ 1,24,500.00`, `Y`/`N`, a title row above the
headings and a **total row underneath**. Both are things the parser has to
survive, and the total row is the one that once arrived as a deal priced at 78.

## `from-ddms/` — what a chartered accountant opens

Thirteen reports per **legal entity**, plus the returns per **registration**.

```
from-ddms/
  SARASWATI/
    01-day-book.csv .. 13-central-end-of-day.csv
    returns-07AAACS1234A1Z5-2026-11/    gstr1-b2b · gstr1-b2cs · gstr3b
  DECCAN/
```

### Every file says the level it was drawn at

R-120, on the first line of each one. A figure whose scope is ambiguous is a
figure somebody will eventually add to another one:

| Question | Level | Files |
|---|---|---|
| Whose money is this? | **entity** | trial balance, P&L, balance sheet, the central end of day |
| Who files this return? | **registration** | GSTR-1, GSTR-3B |
| Where did this happen? | **branch** | day closes, and the branch P&L block |

The day close and the central end of day are the same evening at two levels, and
that is deliberate rather than duplication: a till belongs to a branch and the
cash belongs to a company. The second is a **sum of the first** and is computed
nowhere — it is derived every time it is read, because a stored roll-up quoting
last night's figure is exactly what somebody at head office would quote.

The profit & loss carries both: consolidated, then a block per branch, because a
branch is a profit centre. The **balance sheet is per entity only** — a branch
holds stock and a till belonging to a company that owns them, and a statement
whose assets belong to somebody else is not a statement.

### Three files worth opening first

**`02-trial-balance.csv`** — the title says `DOES NOT BALANCE` if it does not.
Everything else in the folder is worthless if that ever appears.

**`08-stock-transfer-register.csv`** — the *Is a supply?* column. Every
Saraswati row says **No**: same GSTIN, one legal person moving its own stock
between its own branches, so a delivery challan, no tax invoice, no GST and **no
accounting entry at all**. A `YES — taxable` on an internal transfer would mean
the GST return is being inflated every month.

> The *Kind* column carries `VEHICLE` or `PART` since OBJ-46. The daily van to
> the Okhla workshop is on the same document a motorcycle travels on, because it
> is the same movement — and it is the only place in the corpus where the e-way
> column reads `NOT_REQUIRED`, a consignment of brake shoes being a long way
> under fifty thousand rupees.

**`13-central-end-of-day.csv`** — and it says **`does not reconcile`** on every
evening in this fixture, which is the report working rather than failing. Only
two or three of the five branches ever counted a till, and the *Closed?* column
is what makes that visible: a branch that never counted contributes nothing to a
total and reads identically to a branch that counted zero. Two conditions have to
hold before an evening is clean — every branch closed, and every close agreed.

**`12-reconciliations.csv`** — the *Ran?* column is separate from *Clean?* on
purpose. Two of the ten cannot run: nothing imports a bank statement and nothing
imports GSTR-2B. Reporting those as clean would be a tick beside a control
nobody has performed, and the dealership would stop looking.

### No total row here

Deliberately, and it is the one formatting difference from the other side. A
total row exists in a DMS export because the page is printed and signed, and it
is the thing our own parser has to reject as a record. Writing one into our own
output would put a row in a file whose only job is to be thrown away by whoever
reads it next.

### Nothing here computes a figure twice

`export-books.ts` formats and writes; every number comes from the module that
owns it — `trialBalance`, `dayBook`, `salesRegister`, `gstr1For`, `gstr3bFor`,
`ageing`, `reconcileAll`. A report generator with its own arithmetic would be a
second opinion about a number that already has one, and the eighth
reconciliation exists precisely because two sources of one figure eventually
disagree.

## Things in the data worth knowing before you read it

- **Policy numbers carry `SIM-`.** Every one is a policy no insurer issued
  (R-41), and the prefix is on *both* sides of the reconciliation — one side
  carrying it and the other not would manufacture a conflict per matched deal.
- **The deal register has 78 rows and the sales register has more.** The 22
  sales the satellites made never touched the deal mirror: a satellite has no
  DMS account, so it sells through the **typed** path OBJ-30 was built for. That
  the two registers differ is the point, not a defect.
- **Ex-showroom includes GST** (R-122). A bike listed at ₹84,000 invoices at
  ₹84,000; the taxable value is back-calculated out of it and the tax is the
  residual, so the columns always add to the total.
- **One day close is short and one is over**, both named rather than absorbed. A
  close that journalled the difference away would hide the one thing it exists
  to surface.
- **TCS appears nowhere**, and that is correct: it needs an invoice over ₹10
  lakh and Hero's range tops out near ₹2 lakh. A BigWing or a Harley dealership
  meets it; this one does not.
